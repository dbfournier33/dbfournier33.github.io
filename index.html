The problem

Licenses drift. Nobody owns the whole picture.

People leave and their seats keep billing. E5 gets bought where E3 is all anyone uses. Stable headcount sits on monthly terms that cost about 20% more, while volatile headcount gets locked into annual seats that sit empty until renewal. Three teams buy three PDF tools. None of it is anyone's fault. It grows one reasonable decision at a time, and the partner who sells you the licenses has no reason to point it out.

About

Twenty-three years of finding what's hidden.

Descry is the old lookout's verb: to catch sight of what's distant or hidden through trained watching. That has been the founder's job for 23 years, in uniform and out.

Don Fournier spent 15 years as a US Navy Information Systems Technician, the rating created by merging the Radiomen and the Data Processing techs, which meant learning both trades: circuits and message traffic on one watch, servers and networks on the next. He spent eight years earning his anchors and nearly seven as a Chief, running the division. That is where the job description became permanent: find the hidden problems across every system the division touched, and lead the people fixing them.

After a medical retirement, the civilian ladder: systems engineering and identity management in higher education. Deep identity and database work at an identity-software company, where he learned how integrations between systems should actually behave, and how to prove it with queries. A deliberate generalist tour at a medical-device manufacturer: networks, firewalls, servers, virtualization, and a legacy ERP, the systems the production floor ran on. Then a high-volume e-commerce platform, where the ladder went fast. In his first month as a senior sysadmin he unified a cloud and on-prem environment that had been running completely segregated, with no ties between them. He led the infrastructure team. He moved into senior software engineering, where his team rebuilt, in months, an application a vendor had spent years failing to finish, re-architected the e-commerce stack, and swapped the ERP and the commerce platform at the same time without breaking the business. He finished as Director of DevOps and Application Development.

Along the way, licensing kept turning up as the hidden cost. At the e-commerce company he identified roughly $1.5M a year in unused Microsoft licensing out of about $2M in spend, and got leadership sign-off to release them. At the community college, he found about $50k a year in unused Adobe fees. The same habit runs through all of it: read the evidence, price it, and prove it.

Helpdesk to director. Government and corporate. On-prem, cloud, and the messy hybrid in between. Infrastructure and application code. The one constant across all of it: walking into environments where problems were hiding, finding them, and either fixing them or handing someone the plan to. That is not a service Descry invented. It is a career with a company wrapped around it.

The Morse code in our mark spells "D." The lightning bolt is the IT rating's insignia, inherited from the Radiomen. The orange dot is the thing we find. Every element means something. So does every line of our reports.
